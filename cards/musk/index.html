Elon Musk

Elon Musk founded SpaceX, whose Falcon 9 boosters land upright and fly again, cutting launch costs, and led Tesla's push into electric cars. He co-founded OpenAI and later launched xAI, which builds the Grok large language models.

Where the line goes

  1. 2015

    A rocket that comes back

    SpaceX, which Musk founded in 2002, built the Falcon 9 so its first stage flies back and lands upright using engine burns and steerable grid fins. The first successful landing came in December 2015, and boosters now regularly land on droneships at sea and fly again.

  2. the idea

    The hoverslam

    Even at its lowest throttle, a Falcon 9 engine pushes harder than the nearly empty booster weighs, so it cannot hover. The burn must start at exactly the right height: too late and the booster hits the deck too fast, too early and it stops in mid-air and falls. A good burn reaches zero speed at the deck.

  3. today

    Computers that steer, and AI

    No pilot could time this by hand, so onboard guidance computers recalculate the burn and steering many times a second. Musk is also tied to AI directly: he co-founded OpenAI in 2015, left its board in 2018 and launched xAI in 2023, while Tesla trains neural networks on camera video for driver assistance.

Remix the line


      
Lower the ignition altitude until the booster crashes, then raise it until it stops in mid-air; add crosswind and see why lighting a little early can help.

b. 1971Born Pretoria, South AfricaEngineering & Technology Entrepreneurship

    

    
Reusable rockets at SpaceX, Tesla electric cars, and AI ventures.

    
A century after Edison, Musk is the Joker in his mirror: a showman engineer who sells the future, picks public fights, and named his car company after the man Edison battled over AC.

    
The person
Elon Musk grew up in Pretoria, South Africa, moved to Canada at 17 and studied physics and economics at the University of Pennsylvania. He made his first fortune from internet companies, including the business that became PayPal. He founded SpaceX in 2002, and in 2004 became the largest early investor in Tesla, a carmaker named after Nikola Tesla, later becoming its CEO. He co-founded OpenAI in 2015, left its board in 2018, and launched the rival AI company xAI in 2023. He is one of the world's wealthiest and most polarising people.

    
Why they divide opinion
Musk's record includes well-documented controversies. In 2018 the US Securities and Exchange Commission sued him over a tweet about taking Tesla private; he settled, paying a fine and stepping down as Tesla's chairman for three years. After he bought Twitter in 2022 and renamed it X, researchers and journalists reported rises in hate speech and misinformation. His 2025 role leading the US government's Department of Government Efficiency cuts was widely criticised. In 2024 he sued OpenAI over its move towards profit; in May 2026 a jury found his claims were filed too late.

    
The big idea
Rockets were traditionally thrown away after one flight, like flying a jet once and scrapping it. SpaceX engineers built the Falcon 9 so its first stage flies back and lands upright using engine burns and steerable fins, then flies again. The first successful landing came in 2015. Reuse has cut launch costs and made SpaceX the world's busiest launch provider. Tesla, meanwhile, helped make long-range electric cars mainstream, pushing the car industry towards batteries.

    
The line to AI
Musk is tied to AI in several direct ways. He was a co-founder and early funder of OpenAI, which went on to build the GPT models and ChatGPT after he left. His company xAI builds the Grok large language models, trained on a huge cluster of Nvidia GPUs in Memphis. Tesla trains neural networks on camera video for its driver-assistance software and Optimus robot, and Neuralink uses machine learning to decode brain signals from implanted electrodes.

    
On the card
The line of his jaw becomes a Falcon 9 booster's landing trajectory, curving down under a feedback controller, which turns into the loss curve of a neural network descending towards its minimum.

    
Think about
Musk has repeatedly warned about the dangers of AI while also racing to build it. Can a person or company be both a critic and a competitor in the same field? What would you want from them?
