Isaac Newton

Isaac Newton set out the laws of motion and universal gravitation, and developed calculus, the mathematics of change. Training a neural network is a calculus problem: gradient descent follows slopes to nudge millions of weights downhill, and momentum treats them like a rolling Newtonian ball.

Where the line goes

  1. 1687

    A cannon on a mountain

    Newton realised that the pull that drops an apple also holds the Moon. In a thought experiment published with the Principia, he imagined firing a cannon sideways from a very high mountain. A slow ball falls nearby. Each faster ball lands further away, until one falls so far that the curved ground drops away beneath it. It never lands. That is an orbit.

  2. the idea

    Falling all the way round

    Newton's law of gravity can be worked out in small steps: find the pull toward the Earth's centre, nudge the velocity by it, move, and repeat. From a high mountain, a ball slower than about 7.9 km/s hits the ground, one at that speed circles, and one faster than about 11.2 km/s escapes. Stronger gravity raises both speeds.

  3. 1986

    The same nudge trains AI

    Neural networks learn by gradient descent: find the slope of the error and nudge every weight downhill. Adding momentum, popularised for backpropagation in 1986, makes the rule look exactly like the cannonball's: velocity gets nudged by the slope, then position by velocity. The weights roll, overshoot and settle at the minimum.

Laws of motion, universal gravitation, calculus and the nature of light.

    
    
The person
Isaac Newton was born on a farm in Lincolnshire shortly after his father died, and was raised largely by his grandmother. He studied at Trinity College, Cambridge. When the university closed during the plague of 1665 to 1666, he worked at home on calculus, optics and gravity. His Principia (1687) set out the laws of motion and universal gravitation. He showed that white light is a mixture of colours and built the first practical reflecting telescope. He later ran the Royal Mint and was President of the Royal Society.

    
    
The big idea
Newton showed that the same simple rules govern a falling apple and the orbiting Moon. A force changes an object's motion (F = ma), and every mass pulls on every other mass with a force that weakens with the square of the distance. To work with quantities that change continuously, like speed and position, he developed calculus, which Gottfried Leibniz also invented independently. Calculus lets you find the slope of a curve at any point, which tells you how fast something is changing right now.

    
The line to AI
Training a neural network is a calculus problem. The network's error, or loss, is a function of millions of weights, and backpropagation uses derivatives to find which direction each weight should move to reduce it. That is gradient descent. A popular improvement, momentum, treats the weights like a heavy ball rolling downhill under Newtonian dynamics, so it keeps speed through flat regions. Newton's own root-finding method also inspires second-order optimisers that use curvature, not just slope.

    
On the card
The curl of his wig becomes a smooth valley-shaped loss surface, and a ball rolls down it with momentum, its tangent-line slope arrows shrinking as it settles at the minimum (gradient descent).
