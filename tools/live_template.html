Nikola Tesla

The pen never lifts. It draws the man, circles into his rotating field, and runs out toward the way today's language models keep track of word order.

Where the line goes

  1. 1888

    A field that turns

    Tesla patents the AC induction motor. Three out-of-step currents make a magnetic field that rotates, and the rotor simply follows it. The halo behind his head is that field.

  2. phasor

    A wave is a circle seen side-on

    Follow the tip of the spinning arm and read only its height. You get the sine wave of alternating current. Engineers still calculate the grid this way, with rotating arrows called phasors.

  3. 2021

    Position is a rotation

    Rotary position embedding (RoPE) tells a language model where each word sits by turning its vector: word m turns by m × θ. Spin the whole sentence and the angle between any two words stays the same, so the model reads relative position. Many open models, including Llama, use it.

Remix the line


      
Change θ and every word's arrow re-turns. The gap between the two highlighted words stays fixed while the field spins.
