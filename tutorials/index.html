Before we build

We begin with recursion and Seymour Papert: how seeing a rule draw a tree can make an idea understandable. The presenter’s Turtle Art story leads into a short activity with the Lineage cards.

In your group, shuffle 10 cards and lay them face down, including the two jokers, Elon Musk and Rick Rubin. Take turns turning over the cards. The presenter will return to that word later.

The conversation then moves through the MIT example, thinking routines and creativity, and two kinds of problem: one with a known procedure, and one that needs judgement and trial. Rick Rubin introduces the spirit of the workshop: describe what you want, look at what takes shape, and iterate.

What problems do you want to solve?

01 Round 1: pitch a product

  1. Write the most ridiculous word you can think of.
  2. Find someone you do not work with.
  3. Pitch a product with that name.

The aim is to get an idea out into the room and build on it with someone else. This warm-up comes from Creative Hustle by Olatunde Sobomehin and Sam Seidel.

02 Round 2: bring your real problem

  1. Write one real classroom problem in a single sentence.
  2. Find a new partner.
  3. Pitch each other a solution.

Keep that sentence. It is the starting point for the barrier, sketch and prompt you will use next.

03 Find the barrier

A deficit names what the learner cannot do. A barrier names something in the task or design that you can change.

Deficit
They can’t organise their writing.
Barrier
The organising has to happen in their head at the same time as the writing.

Same student. Different problem. Look for the part of your sentence that you can design around.

04 See what can run itself

AutomateApple Shortcuts

Watch the first tool demonstration: Apple Shortcuts, followed by the student notetaking example. Follow the journey from the notes, through the shortcut’s actions, to the response returned to the student.

As you watch, ask: which repeated job in my classroom could become a small automation?

Open the Apple Shortcuts guide

05 Sketch it. Boxes and arrows.

Rewrite your sentence using the barrier idea, then draw the smallest tool that could help. Answer three questions:

  1. What does the student see first?
  2. What can they do on it?
  3. What happens when they tap?

A rough sketch is enough. It gives you something concrete to describe and something to compare with what the AI builds.

06 Write your prompt

Turn the sketch into instructions. Fill the gaps with the learner, barrier and behaviour you have just described.

Your prompt spine

Build a single-screen tool for a Grade ___ student who ___.
Show ___ first, then let them ___.
Keep the language at ___. No sign-in.

Keep this prompt close. You will use it for the first build and return to it when you ask for a change.

07 Build 01

WebAppGemini

After the chatbot demonstration and example, try your own first version. Gemini is the example used in the presentation; the guide takes you from Canvas to building, testing and sharing.

  1. One drives. One talks.
  2. Paste your prompt spine and try the result.
  3. Describe the next change in plain language, then try it again.

Your first version will be wrong. Use that to find out what needs to change; you are exploring whether the idea is buildable.

Open the Gemini WebApp guide

08 Test 01

WebApp

  1. Swap devices with another pair. They use it cold.
  2. You say nothing.
  3. Watch where they hesitate.

Notice what the tool asks people to work out for themselves. Those moments give you a useful next change.

09 Change one thing

WebApp

With your partner, name something you wanted the tool to do that it would not. Use today’s SCAMPER moves to try another approach.

Substitute
Replace one part with something that could work better.
Combine
Bring two useful parts together.
Eliminate
Take something away to make the tool simpler.
Push It!
If yours already works, try a more ambitious version.

Substitute – Combine – Eliminate – Push It! Make one change, then test what happened.

10 Build 02

AgentsXcodeCopilotDeepSeek

The presentation now moves from a WebApp to agents and harnesses. We explore three capabilities: Access Local Files, Create Skills and Automate Workflows. What you can try depends on the tool, its setup and its permissions.

An app is something you open and use. HTML provides the structure; JavaScript provides the behaviour. You describe what the tool should do.

  1. Swap roles: whoever talked now drives.
  2. Same barrier. More ambition.
  3. Use the presenter’s Xcode, Copilot and DeepSeek demonstrations to explore what you could try next.

Nobody finishes this. You are finding out if it is buildable.

11 Continue iterating

Return to the same barrier and the same loop: Barrier → Sketch → Prompt → Build → Test. Keep a useful change, notice the next hesitation, and go round again.

The closing Build 02 slide turns its eight sideways and crosses out “minutes”. It is an invitation to keep exploring, rather than a promise that the work ends with this timer.

Choose a next step you can try with your team. Keep student work safe as you explore, and use sample material while testing a new tool.
